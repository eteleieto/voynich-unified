# Research areas (numbered)

Each area: claim → technique → the area's own verification bar. Agents refer to areas by `A<nn>`.

## A01. The apparent characters are not the real writing units.
- **Technique:** Try different ways of joining or separating visible marks and spaces, using the scans to constrain the choices.
- **Verification bar:** Independent researchers must reproduce the segmentation and obtain better predictions on unseen pages. This verifies useful units, not their meaning.

## A02. Some unusual patterns come from transcription mistakes.
- **Technique:** Compare different transcriptions and inspect disputed passages directly in the images.
- **Verification bar:** Independent readers must confirm the corrected readings, and the supposed anomaly must consistently disappear or shrink after correction.

## A03. One transcription symbol conceals several different signs.
- **Technique:** Group images of apparently identical characters by their actual shapes, then compare where those groups occur.
- **Verification bar:** Other observers must recognize the same distinctions on unseen examples, and those distinctions must predict different textual contexts beyond handwriting differences.

## A04. Different-looking signs are variants of the same character.
- **Technique:** Check whether their shapes depend on neighboring marks or their position within a word or line.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed rule must predict which variant appears in unseen contexts. Identical sound or meaning would still require an independent anchor.

## A05. Small graphic additions systematically modify base signs.
- **Technique:** Separate visible shapes into bases, hooks, bars, and other additions; test whether combinations follow consistent rules.
- **Verification bar:** Independent observers must identify the same components, and the rules must predict combinations not used to construct the model.

## A06. Spaces represent several kinds of boundary.
- **Technique:** Measure gaps and compare their sizes with surrounding characters, line position, and writing style.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed classification must accurately predict unseen boundaries. Calling a particular boundary a “word space” requires additional linguistic evidence.

## A07. Characters or words change form according to line position.
- **Technique:** Compare related forms at line beginnings, middles, and endings; search for a small set of transformations.
- **Verification bar:** Those exact transformations must predict forms on unseen lines without adding new exceptions.

## A08. Large initial “gallows” signs mark structure rather than cipher keys.
- **Technique:** Compare their occurrence with paragraph beginnings, entry types, and decorative conventions.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed rule must predict independently identified structural boundaries. A competing key-switch explanation must additionally demonstrate consistent decoding after each switch.

## A09. Some character sequences are indivisible signs or ligatures.
- **Technique:** Look for recurring sequences that are physically joined and behave like single units.
- **Verification bar:** Image evidence and textual behavior must independently support the same inventory, including on unseen pages.

## A10. A restrictive writing system preserves higher-level information.
- **Technique:** Test whether distant passages help predict text beyond what nearby characters and page identity explain.
- **Verification bar:** Independent reruns must reproduce that extra predictive information against strong comparison models. This establishes structure, not necessarily language.

## A11. Several different tokens encode the same underlying unit.
- **Technique:** Identify forms that appear in nearly interchangeable contexts, then test whether they can be grouped consistently.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed groups must predict unseen substitutions. To establish identical meanings, multiple forms must receive the same independently supported reading.

## A12. Word shapes reflect the sound patterns of a language.
- **Technique:** Test whether hidden consonant-like, vowel-like, or syllable-like classes explain which forms are allowed.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed sound system must predict independently anchored words or grammatical contrasts. Merely finding alternating character classes is insufficient.

## A13. A small set of changing encoding rules transforms ordinary language.
- **Technique:** Test simple encoders that change behavior according to a limited number of states, such as position or preceding signs.
- **Verification bar:** Publish the complete encoder and decoder. Another team must read substantial unseen passages and confirm specific external predictions.

## A14. Much of the text was generated by copying and altering nearby words.
- **Technique:** Simulate writers selecting nearby tokens and changing a few characters. Compare repetition, new-word creation, and page organization.
- **Verification bar:** The same short procedure must reproduce several withheld properties together. This establishes feasibility; historical use and absence of meaning require further evidence.

## A15. A familiar language is written in an unfamiliar practical spelling system.
- **Technique:** Test consistent mappings to known languages while allowing a limited set of unusual spelling conventions.
- **Verification bar:** One fixed mapping must produce grammatical unseen passages and independently corroborated readings, rather than scattered dictionary matches.

## A16. Writers improvised structured but meaningless text.
- **Technique:** Compare the manuscript with long samples of invented writing produced by people under controlled conditions.
- **Verification bar:** Independent experiments must reproduce the combined patterns, including larger-scale organization. Similarity cannot conclusively prove that the manuscript lacks meaning.

## A17. Different scribes used different spelling conventions.
- **Technique:** Compare similar text across proposed hands while controlling for subject and layout.
- **Verification bar:** Small, fixed spelling conversions must predict unseen examples from independently identified hands. Shared meanings need separate confirmation.

## A18. Section differences reflect subject matter or register.
- **Technique:** Compare a shared writing system with different vocabularies against models requiring different keys or languages.
- **Verification bar:** The shared system must predict subject-related variation across unseen, comparable material. Actual subject meanings require independent anchors.

## A19. Writing conventions changed during production.
- **Technique:** Track spelling, abbreviation, and token patterns across physically plausible production sequences.
- **Verification bar:** Changes discovered in one kind of evidence must predict changes in another. Their chronological direction needs physical or documentary support.

## A20. Several repeatable text-generating systems coexist.
- **Technique:** Let a model identify recurring types of text without supplying section or scribe labels first.
- **Verification bar:** The same divisions must recur across reasonable transcriptions and predict unseen regions. Identifying them as languages or keys requires additional evidence.

## A21. Some signs mark formatting rather than lexical content.
- **Technique:** Test whether particular signs indicate divisions or positions, rather than contributing to the represented words.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed rule must predict both their placement and the surrounding text. Claiming that they carry no meaning requires an independently supported reading.

## A22. Line endings contain fillers or special completion forms.
- **Technique:** Measure remaining writing space and test whether it predicts particular endings.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed completion rules must predict unseen endings from available space. Establishing that these forms are semantically redundant requires a reading or close source parallel.

## A23. A simple local rearrangement changes character order.
- **Technique:** Test a small, predetermined set of reversible rearrangements within words or short blocks.
- **Verification bar:** One unchanged rearrangement must support substantial unseen readings and external checks. Different rearrangements chosen for individual words do not qualify.

## A24. Abbreviation occurred before another encoding step.
- **Technique:** Learn abbreviation rules from readable historical examples, then apply candidate encodings to the abbreviated text.
- **Verification bar:** The complete pipeline must predict unseen source-to-manuscript correspondences. Ambiguous expansions must remain unresolved unless independently constrained.

## A25. The text uses a specialist shorthand for recurring expressions.
- **Technique:** Test whether frequent signs stand for whole words, endings, or standard instructions.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed shorthand inventory must correctly expand unseen expressions against independent source passages or identifiable referents.

## A26. A source work survives in the sequence of entry lengths.
- **Technique:** Compare ordered runs of short and long entries with other manuscripts, allowing limited length changes.
- **Verification bar:** A candidate found from one stretch must predict a long withheld continuation and another independent feature. Length agreement alone cannot identify the source.

## A27. The star-marked entries preserve a recipe collection’s order.
- **Technique:** Compare entry lengths, divisions, and repeated formula positions with known practical collections.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed alignment must predict unseen entries and distinctive additional details. Calling it the same text requires actual textual correspondences.

## A28. Repeated source phrases retain recognizable recurrence patterns.
- **Technique:** Compare where recurring sequences appear and how far apart their repetitions occur.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed correspondences must predict withheld recurrence locations and eventually consistent readings. First establish that the proposed encoding would preserve these patterns.

## A29. Rare names or entries retain their positions from a source.
- **Technique:** Compare the arrangement of rare events across candidate texts without initially assigning meanings.
- **Verification bar:** Predict rare-event locations in untouched passages and outperform the best accidental matches from the entire search. Source identity needs independent content evidence.

## A30. The manuscript follows an established compilation format.
- **Technique:** Compare the order and proportions of its subjects with other mixed practical manuscripts.
- **Verification bar:** The proposed format must predict withheld relationships and appear in independent witnesses. This can establish a structural family, not a particular source.

## A31. Plant images and accompanying text derive from one exemplar.
- **Technique:** Align illustration sequences first, then test whether the alignment predicts associated text lengths and divisions.
- **Verification bar:** A frozen image-based alignment must predict unseen textual structure and unusual shared details beyond generic page design.

## A32. The order of plant drawings preserves a herbal lineage.
- **Technique:** Compare drawing sequences using observable features without forcing plant identifications.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed alignment must predict neighboring illustrations and uncommon details not used to select the candidate.

## A33. Circular labels represent an ordered astronomical or calendar list.
- **Technique:** Compare label counts, repetitions, and positions with lists of days, divisions, celestial objects, or similar categories.
- **Verification bar:** One fixed mapping must predict unseen positions and independently identifiable entries. Matching the number of labels is insufficient.

## A34. Circular diagrams use a conventional structural template.
- **Technique:** Compare rings, sectors, connections, and label placement with readable diagrams.
- **Verification bar:** A documented template must predict withheld details. Establishing its intended function also requires close interpreted parallels or corroborating text.

## A35. Entries follow a stable practical-instruction grammar.
- **Technique:** Look for repeating sequences of roles, such as naming, measuring, preparing, and applying.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed role pattern must predict unseen entries. Actual ingredient or action meanings require independently grounded readings.

## A36. Some recurring token classes represent ingredients or substances.
- **Technique:** Compare which tokens occur together with ingredient combinations in known recipe collections.
- **Verification bar:** A locked ingredient mapping must predict new combinations and independent referents. Similar co-occurrence networks alone cannot establish ingredient names.

## A37. Entries are grouped by use or ailment.
- **Technique:** Find persistent vocabulary groups and compare their organization with readable remedy books.
- **Verification bar:** The proposed organization must predict unseen transitions and independently identifiable group meanings. Without those meanings, only clustering is verified.

## A38. Openings and endings share a recoverable conventional formula.
- **Technique:** Test whether the same transformation connects several passage boundaries with known introductory or closing expressions.
- **Verification bar:** It must read withheld boundaries and adjacent text using unchanged rules, matching independently documented wording.

## A39. A stable expansion ratio links source text and encoded text.
- **Technique:** Estimate how much a proposed encoding lengthens or shortens writing, then predict passage lengths.
- **Verification bar:** The mechanism must predict unseen lengths and independently aligned passages. Length prediction alone cannot identify plaintext.

## A40. One language appears in several specialized registers.
- **Technique:** Test whether labels, continuous text, and diagram writing share units but use different arrangements.
- **Verification bar:** One mapping must predict unseen cross-register forms and compatible, independently checked meanings. Shared statistics alone do not establish one language.

## A41. A surviving source was copied with limited omissions or rearrangements.
- **Technique:** Align long passages while allowing only changes calibrated from genuine manuscript copies.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed alignment procedure must predict an untouched continuation and distinctive textual correspondences. Unlimited editing cannot count as a match.

## A42. Images and text independently identify the same source family.
- **Technique:** Select candidate manuscripts using images alone, then evaluate their text relationships separately.
- **Verification bar:** Independent analyses must converge on the same family and predict withheld details without recycling the same assumptions.

## A43. The manuscript translates a known textual tradition.
- **Technique:** Compare organizational features that survive genuine translations, then test candidate language mappings.
- **Verification bar:** One consistent model must predict unseen corresponding passages, with independent specialists confirming relationships beyond broad subject similarity.

## A44. The manuscript is an abridgment of a longer work.
- **Technique:** Look for a consistent policy of retaining particular information and omitting other material.
- **Verification bar:** The policy must predict which unseen source units survive and their actual correspondences. Conveniently deleting every mismatch is not verification.

## A45. Different sections derive from different sources.
- **Technique:** Search for separate source relationships while penalizing every additional source choice.
- **Verification bar:** Each relationship must independently pass source-identification tests on withheld material. Several weak matches do not collectively establish an anthology.

## A46. Plant branching patterns preserve an illustration tradition.
- **Technique:** Compare drawings as branching structures rather than relying on overall visual resemblance.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed features must predict uncommon correspondences and successfully recognize known related herbals. Exact descent requires stronger shared innovations or errors.

## A47. Root drawings retain distinctive exemplar features.
- **Technique:** Compare roots separately from leaves and flowers, testing whether they identify a consistent manuscript family.
- **Verification bar:** The root-based model must predict uncommon features in unseen examples, with independent expert agreement. Generic root shapes are insufficient.

## A48. Some plant components are schematic category markers.
- **Technique:** Look for repeated leaf, stem, or root motifs across otherwise different drawings and compare their associated text.
- **Verification bar:** A predefined motif inventory must predict unseen reuse and text associations. The categories’ meanings need independent support.

## A49. Vessel drawings reflect a restricted artistic or material tradition.
- **Technique:** Compare combinations of vessel necks, feet, handles, and decoration with dated examples.
- **Verification bar:** A proposed tradition must predict rare feature combinations in unseen vessels. A specific location or date requires additional historical corroboration.

## A50. The Rosettes foldout represents relationships among connected centers.
- **Technique:** Compare its network of circles, paths, and labels with geographical, conceptual, and process diagrams.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed interpretation must predict withheld connections and label roles. Naming a particular place or cosmology requires identifiable external referents.

## A51. Zodiac poses preserve an iconographic lineage.
- **Technique:** Compare body positions and unusual attributes with other depictions of the same zodiac subjects.
- **Verification bar:** A selected family must predict uncommon details in several unseen figures, confirmed independently by specialists.

## A52. Gestures have conventional diagrammatic functions.
- **Technique:** Check whether pointing, holding, and body orientation consistently indicate particular relationships.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed gesture rule must predict unseen scenes and work in readable comparison manuscripts. Specific Voynich meanings still need anchors.

## A53. Bathing scenes follow a therapeutic illustration tradition.
- **Technique:** Compare relationships among bodies, vessels, pools, and conduits with medical and nonmedical imagery.
- **Verification bar:** Several rare relationships must identify the same tradition and predict withheld details. A specific treatment requires corroborating text or a close interpreted parallel.

## A54. Some diagrams have identifiable conventional functions.
- **Technique:** Compare their structures with labeled calendar, astronomical, medical, and cosmological diagrams, allowing an “unknown” outcome.
- **Verification bar:** Enumerated correspondences must predict unseen relationships and withstand expert review. A confident image-classifier label is insufficient.

## A55. Color mostly reflects painting practice rather than encoded information.
- **Technique:** Predict coloring from object type, production group, and ordinary artistic habits.
- **Verification bar:** Independently annotated unseen regions must follow the predicted practice. This establishes coloring regularities, not pigment chemistry or precise chronology.

## A56. Overlapping marks reveal the order of text, outlines, and paint.
- **Technique:** Inspect clear crossings to determine which material lies above another.
- **Verification bar:** Independent observers must reproduce each conclusion from identified original-image locations. Ambiguous crossings require better imaging rather than stronger claims.

## A57. Some apparent ink differences are imaging or parchment effects.
- **Technique:** Compare ink tones with nearby parchment, lighting variation, and available material analyses.
- **Verification bar:** Distinct ink batches require independent analytical measurements at the relevant locations. Ordinary color scans alone cannot settle ink composition.

## A58. The manuscript contains several stable handwriting profiles.
- **Technique:** Compare coordinated differences in character shape, proportion, and slant while controlling for context.
- **Verification bar:** Independent specialists and separate measurements must reproduce unseen-page assignments. Distinguishable hands do not automatically identify historical individuals.

## A59. Local handwriting changes mark interruptions in production.
- **Technique:** Find coordinated changes in spacing, slant, and stroke appearance without first consulting vocabulary.
- **Verification bar:** Boundaries found from one feature set must recur in independently measured features. Calling them timed writing sessions needs chronological evidence.

## A60. Spacing reflects learned scribal habits.
- **Technique:** Compare distances between matching character pairs across proposed hands.
- **Verification bar:** Spacing alone must predict independently identified hands across unseen subjects and pages. It cannot directly establish writing speed or pauses.

## A61. The current binding differs from an earlier arrangement.
- **Technique:** Test alternative orders constrained by folds, paired leaves, numbering, and other physical evidence.
- **Verification bar:** A proposed order must satisfy those constraints and predict independent joins or continuations. Equally successful alternatives mean the original order remains unresolved.

## A62. Some boundaries separate distinct production batches.
- **Technique:** Find candidate groups using handwriting, then check layout and textual changes independently.
- **Verification bar:** Several independently collected evidence types must predict the same withheld boundaries. Reusing one classification in several analyses is not independent confirmation.

## A63. Corrections expose rules the writers intended to follow.
- **Technique:** Catalogue secure insertions, overwriting, and changes; look for repeatable before-and-after transformations.
- **Verification bar:** Independent image review must confirm the corrections, and fixed rules must predict withheld examples. Semantic interpretations require additional reading evidence.

## A64. Ruling or planned writing areas explain textual geometry.
- **Technique:** Search for guides and compare their predicted positions with lines and text blocks.
- **Verification bar:** Guides must exist in original or independently acquired images and predict unseen placement. Marks appearing only after one enhancement do not qualify.

## A65. Illustrations reuse templates or copied components.
- **Technique:** Overlay candidate shapes using limited rotations, scaling, and other justified transformations.
- **Verification bar:** Multiple unusual irregularities must align reproducibly. Establishing direct tracing requires evidence beyond resemblance to a shared model.

## A66. Text predicts picture content beyond hand and section.
- **Technique:** Use text to predict specific image features while controlling for production and layout.
- **Verification bar:** Freeze predictions before independent annotators reveal withheld image features. Success establishes content association, not a complete translation.

## A67. Label position distinguishes different referent classes.
- **Technique:** Compare labels attached to roots, leaves, diagram centers, or other independently identified objects.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed position rule must predict unseen label classes. Their names require visible attachment or independently grounded meanings.

## A68. Equivalent diagram positions receive equivalent textual roles.
- **Technique:** Compare text at centers, rings, endpoints, and sectors across diagrams.
- **Verification bar:** A model developed on some diagrams must predict roles on an entirely withheld diagram. Meanings require interpreted parallels or a verified reading.

## A69. Circular text follows a consistent starting and direction convention.
- **Technique:** Derive reading rules from physical cues, then compare the resulting sequences.
- **Verification bar:** Independent readers must obtain the same sequence and predict withheld textual continuities. If several orders fit equally well, the intended order is unresolved.

## A70. Line breaks respect hidden writing units.
- **Technique:** Identify recurring structures that remain intact even when little space remains.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed unit inventory must predict unseen breaks and nonbreaks beyond ordinary spacing and width effects. This verifies a layout constraint, not pronunciation.

## A71. Labels and running text share vocabulary units.
- **Technique:** Learn recurring forms from continuous text and test whether they explain labels under limited adjustments.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed mappings must reproduce unseen label forms and their running-text counterparts directly from the scans. Shared meaning needs additional evidence.

## A72. Star markers delimit entries with consistent internal structure.
- **Technique:** Compare beginnings and endings relative to stars rather than ordinary line positions.
- **Verification bar:** Text-based predictions must locate unseen entry boundaries confirmed independently from images. Establishing that the entries are recipes requires further evidence.

## A73. Text length depends on depicted information, not just available space.
- **Technique:** Compare text amount with image complexity while accounting for drawing size and remaining writing area.
- **Verification bar:** Content features must improve predictions on unseen pages beyond a complete layout baseline. This alone does not prove descriptive prose.

## A74. Similar depicted subjects share specific textual patterns.
- **Technique:** Select similar image pairs without seeing their text, then compare their wording or token families.
- **Verification bar:** Predicted relationships must hold for unseen pairs and identifiable ink sequences. A proposed word meaning must distinguish similar but different subjects.

## A75. One manuscript family shares visual and organizational conventions.
- **Technique:** Retrieve candidates using one kind of evidence, then evaluate different features independently.
- **Verification bar:** A feature-by-feature comparison must predict withheld details and survive expert review and unrelated controls. General visual similarity is insufficient.

## A76. Some apparent regional matches result from uneven digitization.
- **Technique:** Repeat comparisons after balancing the number, subject, age, and image quality of manuscripts from different regions.
- **Verification bar:** Publish the sampling choices and reproduce the result. A regional attribution is supported only if it survives balancing and gains external corroboration.

## A77. Apparent topic groups mainly reflect production groups.
- **Technique:** Compare page groupings based separately on text, images, handwriting, and physical adjacency.
- **Verification bar:** Production factors must predict unseen associations, and remaining variation must be checked for content. If subject and hand cannot be separated, the cause stays unresolved.

## A78. Shared illustration errors reveal common ancestry.
- **Technique:** Search for uncommon deviations repeated in the same order across manuscript witnesses.
- **Verification bar:** Independent specialists must confirm several linked correspondences and predict withheld details. Direct copying requires evidence beyond common ancestry.

## A79. Flexible analysis creates some apparent meanings.
- **Technique:** Repeat interpretations with blinded labels, fixed rules, complete example sets, and negative controls.
- **Verification bar:** Another team must reproduce the finding without knowing the desired interpretation, including rejected and unsuccessful examples. This verifies robustness, not meaning by itself.

## A80. Different sections represent different languages.
- **Technique:** Compare multiple-language explanations with one-language models allowing different subjects and writing conventions.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed mappings must produce substantial grammatical unseen passages in each language and agree on shared referents. Clusters or language-classifier guesses cannot establish this.

## A81. A small construction table generates allowable word forms.
- **Technique:** Combine a limited inventory of beginnings, cores, and endings; compare the generated text with the manuscript.
- **Verification bar:** An independent implementation must reproduce several withheld properties together without storing every observed word. Historical use and meaning remain separate questions.

## A82. The text’s errors indicate copying from an exemplar.
- **Technique:** Compare secure omissions, repetitions, and substitutions with controlled copying and composition experiments.
- **Verification bar:** A method validated on known writing processes must predict unseen error types. Identifying a particular exemplar needs inherited errors or an actual source.

## A83. Near-duplicate passages descend from shared formulae.
- **Technique:** Reconstruct a common template and a small set of changes explaining related passages.
- **Verification bar:** The fixed template must predict withheld variants. Exact ancestral wording or copying direction requires an external witness or informative physical correction.

## A84. Pen or instrument changes explain local stroke differences.
- **Technique:** Compare manuscript variation with controlled examples of re-inking, changing pen angle, and changing instruments.
- **Verification bar:** Patterns must match independent calibration and survive imaging controls. If several causes look the same, the particular cause remains unresolved; no writing-time “clock” follows.

## A85. The text contains prompts for trained readers rather than full instructions.
- **Technique:** Compare its information distribution with readable shorthand notes, mnemonic prompts, and complete recipes.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed expansion scheme must predict missing information in independently interpretable parallels. Similarity to notes cannot establish what readers supplied mentally.

## A86. Pages organize information into recurring fields.
- **Technique:** Test whether stable positions contain recurring kinds of information rather than continuous sentences.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed field model must identify unseen regions beyond ordinary layout effects. Names such as “dose” or “properties” require independent interpretation.

## A87. First or last signs mark structural information.
- **Technique:** Test whether edge signs predict grammatical divisions, entry types, or notation boundaries.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed inventory must predict independently identified unseen boundaries. Any hidden-message claim additionally needs a fixed extraction rule and externally checkable content.

## A88. Local page layout determines reading order.
- **Technique:** Apply a small set of adjacency and orientation rules to diagram text, excluding arbitrary paths.
- **Verification bar:** Independent readers must derive identical orders and successful unseen continuity predictions. Several equally successful paths leave the intended order unverified.

## A89. Diagram geometry indexes textual categories.
- **Technique:** Predict text classes from rings, sectors, or branches before examining token identities.
- **Verification bar:** One indexing convention must predict unseen diagram–text relationships, including difficult counterexamples. Category meanings need independent anchors.

## A90. Images supply information omitted from the text.
- **Technique:** Look for repeated text templates associated with different images that may supply the missing referent.
- **Verification bar:** A fixed template must predict omitted slots and their image relationships on unseen pages. Exact referents need external parallels or independent identification.

## A91. Foldout configurations organize how the manuscript was used.
- **Technique:** Reconstruct physically supported folded states and examine which diagrams and text become visible together.
- **Verification bar:** Independent reconstruction must reproduce predicted relationships using documented folds and dimensions. Intended use needs corroborating wear, labels, or functional parallels.

## A92. Missing leaves explain some abrupt changes.
- **Technique:** Compare textual discontinuities with independently documented losses and intact joins.
- **Verification bar:** Physical evidence of loss must precede the textual analysis, and the model must predict withheld continuity patterns. Missing wording needs a surviving witness to be verified.

## A93. Some colors serve as category or reference marks.
- **Technique:** Test whether color predicts textual roles after accounting for object type and painting habits.
- **Verification bar:** One fixed color rule must predict unseen examples, especially contrasting cases involving similar objects. Pigment identities require material measurements.

## A94. Some signs represent numbers, units, or enumerators.
- **Technique:** Search for consistent quantity–unit arrangements, ordered series, and constrained numerical relationships.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed values must satisfy several independent equations, sequences, or externally checkable counts in unseen material. Position and frequency alone are insufficient.

## A95. The writing collapses distinctions present in the source language.
- **Technique:** Test systems that merge sounds, omit endings, or otherwise discard information consistently.
- **Verification bar:** Fixed rules must predict independently anchored unseen forms and grammar. Where several plaintexts remain possible, a unique reading requires further evidence.

## A96. Some tokens belong to a semantic codebook.
- **Technique:** Test whether units represent whole substances, actions, or concepts rather than individual sounds.
- **Verification bar:** A locked codebook must predict unseen combinations and independently checkable referents. Assigning meanings after inspecting each image does not qualify.

## A97. Base signs and added marks encode separate properties.
- **Technique:** Test whether a base represents one dimension and a modifier another, allowing meanings to combine systematically.
- **Verification bar:** Independent examples must anchor both dimensions, after which the same rule must predict unseen combinations and interpretations.

## A98. A proposed reading predicts visual facts concealed during decoding.
- **Technique:** Hide relevant illustrations, decode their associated text using fixed rules, and predict exact counts, positions, or attributes.
- **Verification bar:** Record predictions before independent scoring and image revelation. Success corroborates those readings; the complete decipherment must still pass the broader requirements.

## A99. The same writer or training tradition appears in other manuscripts.
- **Technique:** Compare unusual writing habits and comparable strokes, then check dates and provenance.
- **Verification bar:** Blinded specialists must confirm several rare shared habits and historical compatibility. Identifying the same person requires stronger evidence than general stylistic resemblance.

## A100. Meaningful material coexists with repetitive or nonsemantic expansion.
- **Technique:** Define a fixed rule separating a meaningful core from padding or mechanically generated additions, then test both parts independently.
- **Verification bar:** The rule must predict unseen expansions and produce independently corroborated core readings. Only the verified core counts as decoded unless the remaining material’s function is also established.
